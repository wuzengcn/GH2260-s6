Distinction between Health and Medical Care

What is health?

Health is the desired state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being, and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity.

What is medical care?

Medical care is a reactive service used to diagnose, treat, and manage specific diseases or injuries.

Derived demand

Derived demand for health care

Demand for health care is derived from the demand for health

  • It is not medical care that consumers want, but rather health
  • Consumers demand medical care inputs to produce it
  • Health can be treated both as a consumption good and an investment good (capital good)
  • As we demand for health, we are interested in what is the most efficient way to produce health

Production of health

Health and health investment

Health is a stock and capital good (like buildings and cars):

  • \(H_{jt}\): Health stock of individual \(j\) at time \(t\).

Health investment (e.g., medical care, exercise, nutrition) increases the stock of health:

  • \(I_{jt}\): Health investment of individual \(j\) at time \(t\).

Health depreciates over time, similar to physical capital: Assuming a depreciation rate at \(\delta\)

  • \(\delta H_{jt}\): The health stock depreciation of individual \(j\) at time \(t\).

The health stock of individual \(j\) at time \(t+1\) is given by: - \(H_{j,t+1} = H_{jt} + I_{jt} - \delta H_{jt} \rightarrow \Delta H_{jt} = H_{j,t+1} - H_{j,t} = I_{jt} - \delta H_{jt}\)

What goes in to the health investment function?

  • \(I_{jt} = f(MC_{jt}, T_{jt}, C_{jt}, A_{jt}, B_{jt})\)
    • \(MC_{jt}\): Medical care inputs (e.g., physician visits, hospital stays, prescription drugs)
    • \(T_{jt}\): Time devoted to the production of health
    • \(C_{jt}\): Human capital (e.g. education, knowledge, skills)
    • \(A_{jt}\): Age
    • \(B_{jt}\): Behavioral risk factors (e.g., smoking, alcohol consumption, diet, exercise)

Measurement of health


Health as “a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity.”

  • Mortality
    • Infant mortality
    • Maternal mortality
    • Adult mortality
    • Life expectancy
  • Morbidity
    • Incidence
    • Prevalance
    • Disability
  • Combined indicator
    • QALY: Quality-adjusted life years
    • DALY: Disability-adjusted life years

Public health, Nutrition and Health

  • Public health and nutrition are important determinants of health status
    • Vaccine
    • Water and sanitation
  • Nutrition is a key determinant of health status
    • Malnutrition is a major cause of morbidity and mortality in developing countries
    • Obesity is a major cause of morbidity and mortality in developed countries

Demand for medical care

  • The demand for medical care is a derived demand, resulting from the demand for good health

  • Demand for medical care has a consumption component and an investment component

  • Demand for medical care and its price are inversely related

Demand based on need and willingness to pay

Determinant of demand for medical care

\(M_{jt} = f(H_{jt}, D_{jt}, E_{jt}, P_{t})\)

  • \(M_{jt}\): Medical care demand of individual \(j\) at time \(t\)
  • \(H_{jt}\): Health stock of individual \(j\) at time \(t\)
  • \(D_{jt}\): Demographic characteristics of individual \(j\) at time \(t\)
  • \(E_{jt}\): Economic characteristics of individual \(j\) at time \(t\)
  • \(P_{t}\): Physician factors at time \(t\)

Economic standing (health insurance)

The above demand analysis assumed that the patient pays for care out-of-pocket

How does insurance affect the demand for care?

  • Coinsurance: Patient pays only a fixed % of the cost of each visit (often C = .20)
  • If the visit costs $100 : patient pays $20, insurance pays $80

Health insurance

  • No insurance: consumer faces price \(P_0\), makes \(Q_0\) visits
  • With coinsurance: consumer faces price \(P_1\), wants to make \(Q_1\) visits
  • Coinsurance leads to a demand of \(Q_1\) visits at price \(P_0\), shared by consumer and insurance company Demand curve rotates clock wise

Physician factors

principal-agent relationship

  • Principal-agent relationship:
    • Fundamentally it is a problem of asymmetric information
      • Physician (agent) has more information than the patient (principal)
      • Physician can make decisions on behalf of the patient (principal)
  • Consequence of the principal-agent relationship:
    • Physician can influence the demand for medical care
    • Physician can induce demand for medical care

Supply induced demand

Supply induced demand

Is supply induced demand a problem?

Evidence is mixed

Potential implications

  • Supply induced demand is probably not as widespread as it was once thought to be

  • With the new payment mechnisms, capitation and bundled payments, supply induced demand is likely to be less of a problem

  • Patients, payers and lawmakers become more knowledgeable about the medical practices and procedure, SID will be less of a concern

Demand for medical care: elasticity

Price elasticity: the percentage change in quantity demanded resulting from a 1% change in price

  • Most inelastic
  • More elatic for outpatient than hosptial care
  • More elastic for preventive than hospital care

Income Elaticity

Income elasticity: the percentage change in quantity demanded resulting from a 1% change in income

  • Vary substantially
  • Mixed: (luxury vs. necessity goods)

Cross-price elasticity: the percentage change in quantity demanded of one good resulting from a 1% change in the price of another good

Policy implications of demand elasticity

1

Price elasticity of demand for medical care is generally inelastic, but varies by type of care and patient characteristics

2

Some medical care is more elastic than others, and some patients are more sensitive to price changes than others
